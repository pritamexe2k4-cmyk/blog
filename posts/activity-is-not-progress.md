SPIDER Journal is a daily honest log of what I asked for, what happened, and real progress on a two-month AI job pipeline. The rules are short:

- No motivational fluff
- Separate activity from progress
- Note when we over-built infrastructure instead of applications

I am putting that on the public blog so the rule has somewhere to live outside a private page.

## What counts

Progress is: a resume that is locked, an application that was sent, a RAG eval that refuses correctly, a POST that returns a typed result, a GATE DA section actually worked.

Activity is: another tracker, another folder, another README, another agent that does not run.

I have a public LaTeX resume at [pritamexe2k4-cmyk/resume](https://github.com/pritamexe2k4-cmyk/resume), a RAG design at [production-rag](https://github.com/pritamexe2k4-cmyk/production-rag), and an agents repo at [agentic-ops](https://github.com/pritamexe2k4-cmyk/agentic-ops). Those are artifacts. They are not offers.

## Cadence I will keep in public

Engineering notes ship here when they are specific: a pipeline, a refusal rule, a contract. They do not ship when they are mood. Daily journal entries stay in Notion. If a day produced only infrastructure, the journal says so. This blog should say so too.

The job pipeline still has a simple honesty check: applications sent this week, resume locked or not, code that a stranger can run. If those are empty, the week was activity.

## Why a blog at all

Recruiters and future teammates do not read SPIDER Journal. They can read this. The writing should look like the work: cite the repo, show the endpoint, admit what is not shipped. If a post cannot point at a file, it is not ready.
